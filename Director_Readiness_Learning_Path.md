# Director Readiness Learning Path

Purpose: turn the material in this folder into a practical path toward a Director-level role in Operations, CX, Customer Success, Support, or Account Management.

The goal is not just to "study" the files. The goal is to build Director habits: diagnose business problems, quantify impact, design systems, lead through people, communicate tradeoffs, and show measurable results.

## Mastery Standard

You are ready to apply when you can do four things without notes:

1. Explain the business model, margin structure, and account profitability of a staffing or services account.
2. Diagnose customer/account health using financial, operational, support, and relationship signals.
3. Build a practical operating system: KPIs, cadence, SOPs, escalation paths, staffing logic, dashboards, and follow-through.
4. Tell strong interview stories with quantified impact, clear decisions, stakeholder management, and lessons learned.

## Path Overview

Recommended pace: 12 focused weeks plus a 4-week capstone.

Weekly time budget:

- 2 hours reading or watching the source material
- 2 hours workbook/dashboard practice
- 1 hour writing executive summaries
- 1 hour interview-story practice
- 1 hour applying one idea at work

If you can do 10+ hours per week, add more practice cases. If you can do only 4 hours, keep the weekly deliverable and reduce reading depth.

### Week 1: Staffing Economics and Margin

Learn:

- Client rate vs cost to company
- Gross margin percent
- Monthly profit
- ARR
- Healthy vs risky margin ranges
- Loss-making account signals

Practice:

- Rebuild the examples from `Phase_1_Financial_Acumen_Guide.docx` by hand.
- Explain why a 10% margin account is dangerous even if revenue exists.
- Calculate margin and monthly profit for every worker in a simple table.

Deliverable:

- One-page cheat sheet: "How this business makes money."
- 5-minute verbal explanation: "How I diagnose account profitability."

Mastery check:

- Given worker rate, cost, and hours, you can calculate monthly revenue, monthly cost, gross profit, gross margin, and ARR in under 5 minutes.

### Week 2: Present Value, NPV, and Investment Logic

Learn:

- Future value
- Present value
- Simple vs compound interest
- Discount rate
- NPV rule
- Why a project can look good in nominal dollars but bad after discounting

Practice:

- Work through `LectureNotesT01.pdf`, `LectureNotesT02.pdf`, and `NPVRule.pdf`.
- Use `NPV_Calculator.xlsx` to test one-year and multi-year cash flows.
- Use `Advanced_NPV_Model.xlsx` to compare worst, realistic, and best case scenarios.

Deliverable:

- One executive memo: "Should we approve this initiative?"
- Include assumptions, cash flows, discount rate, NPV, risks, and recommendation.

Mastery check:

- You can explain NPV to a non-finance stakeholder using a workplace example.

### Week 3: Rate Negotiation and Business Impact

Learn:

- Rate increase impact
- Discount impact
- Cost leakage
- Margin protection
- Scenario analysis

Practice:

- Create three rate-change scenarios: client asks for 5% discount, client accepts 7% increase, and scope expands without billing.
- Model the annual profit impact.
- Use the investment simulators to reinforce compounding and decision tradeoffs.

Deliverable:

- "Rate Negotiation Impact Calculator" in Excel or Google Sheets.
- One talking track for pushing back on discount requests.

Mastery check:

- You can defend a pricing recommendation using numbers, not opinions.

## Phase 4: Operational Excellence, WFM, and Standardization

Weeks 8-9

Main sources:

- `How to achieve operational excellence.jpg`
- `Required hrs in WFM.jpg`
- `Standardization.gif`

### Week 8: Operating System Design

Learn:

- Simplifying the mission
- Leading and lagging indicators
- Systems over individual heroics
- Psychological safety plus accountability
- Cross-functional alignment
- Weekly follow-through

Practice:

- Pick one real workflow from work.
- Document current state, pain points, failure modes, and owners.
- Create a weekly operating cadence: KPI review, blockers, decisions, owner, deadline.

Deliverable:

- Operating Cadence Template.
- Process Audit: current state, root causes, proposed future state.

Mastery check:

- You can identify where a problem is caused by process design, unclear ownership, poor incentives, or missing data.

### Week 9: Workforce Management and Standard Work

Learn:

- Required hours = forecasted volume x AHT / 3600
- Forecast inputs
- Overstaffing and understaffing
- SOPs
- Standardization as quality control

Practice:

- Build a required-hours calculator using the WFM formula.
- Add scenarios for higher volume, lower AHT, shrinkage, occupancy, and service level assumptions.
- Write one SOP for a repeated workflow.

Deliverable:

- WFM Required Hours Calculator.
- One clean SOP with owner, trigger, steps, exception path, KPI, and review cadence.

Mastery check:

- You can connect staffing levels to customer experience, cost, and service quality.

  - Trend over time
  - Segment comparison

Deliverable:

- One-page data story: "What changed, why it matters, what we should do."

Mastery check:

- You can look at a chart and say what decision it should support.

## Weekly Routine

Use this every week:

1. Read the assigned source.
2. Extract 10 key ideas.
3. Build one artifact.
4. Explain it out loud in 5 minutes.
5. Apply one idea at work.
6. Write one quantified bullet or interview story.

## Director-Level Questions to Practice

- What are the three metrics that best show account health?
- Which account would you grow, fix, renegotiate, or exit?
- How would you reduce churn without increasing headcount?
- How would you improve CSAT if ticket volume is rising?
- How would you decide whether a new initiative is worth funding?
- How would you calculate staffing needs for a support queue?
- Where would you standardize work, and where would you allow flexibility?
- How do you balance empathy for customers with boundaries for the team?
- How do you turn raw data into an executive decision?
- What would your first 90 days as Director look like?

## Final Readiness Checklist

- I can calculate margin, monthly profit, ARR, and rate-change impact.
- I can explain NPV and use it in a business recommendation.
- I have an account profitability dashboard.
- I have four case studies with quantified impact.
- I understand CS, support, and account management as different but connected functions.
- I can define customer health, support KPIs, and retention/expansion signals.
- I have a Zendesk/support SOP and response playbook.
- I can calculate WFM required hours.
- I can build a simple executive dashboard with lookup logic.
- I can write a 30/60/90 Director plan.
- I have six Director-level stories ready for interviews.

## Best Starting Point

Start with the financial acumen guide and the account case-study files. Those are the strongest bridge between your current operational/CX background and Director-level business judgment.

Then layer in Customer Success and Support leadership, then operational excellence, then analytics and AI. That order matters: numbers first, customer/account system second, execution system third, productivity and polish fourth.
